What my weeks look like
My work doesn't fit a clean job description. Here's what it actually looks like.

Fundraising & investors

Finding the right investors, studying their portfolio patterns, writing messages that get responses, and keeping conversations alive. Half the skill is knowing who not to waste time on.

Finance & models

Budgets, projections, unit economics. My training is in finance, so this is where I'm sharpest. I can look at a model, find what's wrong, and fix it before an investor does.

Bringing in the right people

When I hit something I can't solve alone, I find the person who can. I've connected with advisors who have deep experience in our space. I'd rather bring in the right help than pretend I have every skill myself.

Reading people & patterns

Why did that investor pass? What made that customer respond? What's the real objection behind the polite no? I study behaviour, I spot patterns, and I use that to make better calls next time.

Outreach that actually works

I write every cold email and message myself. Personalised, researched, specific. Most of our pipeline started because I wrote something that made someone want to reply. That's preparation, not luck.

Operations & problem solving

Some days it's restructuring a database. Other days it's working out how to get the right meeting at an event in Hong Kong. Startups don't have neat job scopes. I'm fine with that.

How I Work
Principles I work by

Full responsibility, always

If I took it on, the result is mine. Not "I tried." Not "I was waiting on someone." Good outcome or bad, it's on me. I don't share blame and I don't make excuses.

Gaps are problems to solve

I don't know everything. But I've never let that stop a result. I learn, I ask, I pull in someone who's been there. The thing still gets delivered. The gap just means I have to be more resourceful.

Build the team, not just the task

One person can't carry every function. So I bring in advisors, build relationships with people who know more than me, and make sure the right expertise is there when it matters. The job is making sure the team wins.

Think in patterns

Why did that investor pass? What made that customer engage? What's the real signal in the noise? I study behaviour, notice what repeats, and use it to make sharper decisions next time. That's how you get better fast.
